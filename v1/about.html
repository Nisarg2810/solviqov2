About Solviqo

We exist because the backlog was the real problem.

Not the ERP. Not the operations team. The six months it took to get a small, obviously necessary tool built, while the actual work got tracked on paper and in group chats in the meantime.

Solviqo started from a specific, repeated observation, not a business plan. Building operational tools inside companies that run Oracle Fusion and similar ERPs, the pattern was always the same. The platform could technically do almost anything. Getting it to do one specific, small thing, a violation tracker, a room assignment log, a fleet inspection form, took months, because that request was competing for attention against module rollouts worth a hundred times more.

In the meantime, the actual operation kept running anyway. On a spreadsheet five people had access to. In a WhatsApp thread that counted as an approval record. On paper, in a folder, in a site office. The system of record existed. It just was not where the work actually happened.

Solviqo is the studio built to close that specific gap: one workflow at a time, fast enough that waiting stops being the default, and connected to the ERP you already run instead of asking you to replace it.

How we operate

Four principles, held the same way on every project.

PRINCIPLE 01

Precise, not clever

Every scope is written down before we start: what gets built, what it does on day one, and when it ships. No adjectives standing in for a delivery date.

PRINCIPLE 02

Engineered, not experimental

Role based access, a real database, a documented integration. We build the way a systems integrator ships to spec, not the way a prototype gets duct taped together.

PRINCIPLE 03

Name the actual gap

If the problem is a six month Oracle Fusion queue or an approval chain living in WhatsApp, we say that plainly. Most of the pitch is just naming what everyone already knows.

PRINCIPLE 04

Confident, not loud

No exclamation points, no claim of reinventing anything. A fixed price and a working app in four weeks carries its own argument.
